You've got beamtime, but what do you do now?

Congratulations on getting your proposal accepted! We look forward to seeing you at one of our instruments soon. However, there are some steps you'll need to complete before you come here.

You should follow through the following checklist:

  • Have a discussion with your local contact
  • Prepare your samples for the experiment
  • Confirm the correct sample environment and sample holder
  • Ensure the correct q-range is selected
  • Prepare your risk assessment, both the one to be submitted online and the paper one for any lab work at ISIS

Conversation with a local contact

Before you come to ISIS, you should have a conversation with your local contact. This is the person who will be helping you with your experiment and will be your main point of contact for any questions or concerns. This is particularly important when using a non-standard sample environment, and is helpful if you are unsure about any aspect of your experiment. Please note that this does not stop after the experiment, should you need help with data reduction and data analysis you can also ask questions after the experiment. The important things to talk to your local contact before the experiment are as follows:

  • What is the goal of your experiment?
  • What sample environment will you be using?
  • What is the optimal q-range for your experiment?
  • Any other sample or setup related concerns/questions.

How should I make my samples?

ISIS SANS measurements can typically supports various forms of matter, for instance our standard sample changers can handle liquids, solids, and powders. However, we can also make use of high pressure environments for gaseous samples. The key is that your local contact is aware of this, and it was noted during the proposal submission.

What is my q-range and why is it important?

The q-range refers to the range of momentum transfer values that can be accessed in a single measurement, which determines the lengthscales accessible in the experiment. Therefore, it is important to discuss this and choose the correct configuration for the experiment, to capture all of the lengthscales you are interested in probing. Below is a table containing the q-range for each instrument in each configuration, and a graphic enabling you to visualise the size to q-range relationship.

Safety is a priority not a compromise

There is an important aspect in any practical science environment, which is risk assessments. We understand that risk assessments might be time-consuming and/or tedious. However, they are also extremely important and can be the key in an emergency. At ISIS, we have two main risk assessments that you need to take care to prepare. The first is before the experiment, and is the one submitted online. This must be submitted at least 2 weeks before your experiment. The other risk assessment to be engaged with is the handwritten ones in the labs when you arrive, make sure that these can be read/understood by everyone and are legible.
