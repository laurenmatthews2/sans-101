Writing a SANS beamtime proposal? Start here

If you apply for beamtime on one of our SANS instruments at ISIS, you have roughly a 40% chance of being awarded time. All SANS proposals are reviewed by a panel with a very wide range of expertise and limited time. With this in mind, a strong proposal makes the science clear regardless of the reviewer’s background, explicitly justifies the need for neutrons, and outlines a concise, realistic experimental plan. This page walks you through how proposals are judged, what to prepare, how to structure your two-page science case, and concludes with a highlighted template you can download and use.

Before you start writing, work through this checklist:

  1. Understand who will read your proposal, and how little time they have
  2. Choose the right route: Direct, Rapid, Xpress or ICRD
  3. Talk to an instrument scientist early
  4. Pick the instrument and check the q-range
  5. Calculate the beamtime you can justify
  6. Gather what the online form asks for
  7. Write the science case (two sides of A4), using the template
  8. Check it against the do's and don'ts

Who reads your proposal, and how?

Proposals for the ISIS facility are reviewed by Facility Access Panels or FAPs, which consist of multi-disciplinary groups of external academics and industrial scientists, whose membership rotates roughly every three years. To moderate these panels, two ISIS staff act as a secretary and technical representative. Remember this: your proposal has to be clear to a fellow scientist who is not a specialist in your field.

What happens to a proposal

  1. Technical checkLocal contacts assess feasibility and recommend the instrument and sample environment.
  2. Two assessorsTwo panel members read the science and each give a score out of 10.
  3. A championOne of the two assessors presents and champions your proposal to the rest of the panel.
  4. Short discussionUnder 5 minutes, then the panel ranks against the cut-off.

How much beamtime can you justify?

The panel wants to see exactly how the days were arrived at: samples × temperatures × contrasts × time per run = days. Add a block for each part of the experiment, and the calculator totals the hours and writes a sentence for your proposal. Counts can be written as products, for example 6*2*3*4.

What the online form asks for

The online proposal is a 10-step process that gathers what ISIS needs for the experiment and for administration. Gather your experimental team, your grants, previous publications and sample details for the safety assessment before you start. The science case itself is a 2-page document uploaded at step 9, so write it in advance.

Do's and don'ts

Reviewers will see your proposal reformatted: scaled down and in black and white. Try it below.

Do

  • Read the guidance on the ISIS website, because things change.
  • Follow the suggested structure. The information is needed for both the technical and the scientific assessment.
  • Contact an instrument scientist well in advance if you are unsure about instrument choice or sample environment options.
  • Be clear about the q-ranges you need and the length scales you expect.

Don't

  • Use tiny fonts or squeeze in too much. Use at least 11 pt.
  • Forget that proposals are reformatted to 70% of their original size, so think about figures, captions and legends.
  • Rely on colour in figures, because they are reformatted to black and white.
  • Assume too much knowledge. The panel covers a huge range of science and cannot have specialists in everything.

Text set at 11 pt. At 70% it is the equivalent of about 7.7 pt, so anything smaller to begin with becomes very hard to read. Captions and legends need the same care as body text.

In black and white the three curves and their legend look the same. Use different markers or line styles, and label curves directly.
