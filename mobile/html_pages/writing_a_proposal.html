Writing a SANS beamtime proposal? Start here

If you apply for beamtime on one of our SANS instruments at ISIS, you have roughly a 40% chance of being awarded time. All SANS proposals are reviewed by a panel with a very wide range of expertise and limited time. With this in mind, a strong proposal makes the science clear regardless of the reviewer’s background, explicitly justifies the need for neutrons, and outlines a concise, realistic experimental plan. This page walks you through how proposals are judged, what to prepare, how to structure your two-page science case, and lets you build the science case and generate it as a PDF.

Before you start writing, work through this checklist:

  1. Choose the right route: Direct, Rapid, Xpress or ICRD
  2. Talk to an instrument scientist early
  3. Pick the instrument and check the q-range
  4. Calculate the beamtime you can justify
  5. Build the science case (two sides of A4) and generate it as a PDF
  6. Check it against the do's and don'ts

Talk to an instrument scientist early

Instrument scientists are here to assist with the process, they DO NOT write the proposal for you. But they are able to give you hints and tips specific to your proposed science area, they are able to also provide further technical information for the proposal. This will not only help you fully plan your experiment, but it will also help clarify for the panel and make the instrument scientist’s job easier once you are on site for your beamtime. Here is a non-exhaustive list of points you should discuss with an instrument scientist prior to proposal submission.

Enter the largest object or correlation length and the smallest feature you need to resolve. This uses qmin ≈ π / Dmax and qmax ≈ 2π / dmin as a first guide only. Be explicit about your q-range and length scales in the proposal.

Do's and don'ts

Reviewers will see your proposal reformatted: scaled down and in black and white. Try it below.

Do

  • Read the guidance on the ISIS website, because things change.
  • Follow the suggested structure. The information is needed for both the technical and the scientific assessment.
  • Contact an instrument scientist well in advance if you are unsure about instrument choice or sample environment options.
  • Be clear about the q-ranges you need and the length scales you expect.

Don't

  • Use tiny fonts or squeeze in too much. Use at least 11 pt.
  • Forget that proposals are reformatted to 70% of their original size, so think about figures, captions and legends.
  • Rely on colour in figures, because they are reformatted to black and white.
  • Assume too much knowledge. The panel covers a huge range of science and cannot have specialists in everything.

Text set at 11 pt. At 70% it is the equivalent of about 7.7 pt, so anything smaller to begin with becomes very hard to read. Captions and legends need the same care as body text.

In black and white the three curves and their legend look the same. Use different markers or line styles, and label curves directly.
